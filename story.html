Story

From a McDonald's
counter to this.

I'm Roman Toilolo. I'm 21, I grew up an athlete, and I've spent the last few years learning one thing in a lot of hard ways: how to make a business grow. Here's how I got here.

01 — The start

A stranger, a job, and a lesson in business.

At 18 I was a college athlete just trying to make money. I was sitting in a McDonald's filling out an application when a man walked up to me. He owned a private security company, and he asked if I wanted work.

I worked with him until I was 19. He took me to Los Angeles and taught me the things school doesn't — how to communicate, how to market, how to actually talk to people about business. That was my first real education, and it stuck.

02 — Learning to sell

10+ carriers. Four months. A hard truth.

From there I got into life insurance — an independent agent selling through more than ten different companies. My first month I did three deals and made $5,000. The next three months were $10,000 months.

But I was also a full-time student and a full-time athlete, and the cold calling and telemarketing drained me mentally. So I made a call: I put it down and focused on school and sports, studying business marketing. Walking away taught me as much as the money did.

03 — The reset

Injured, broke, and researching at 2am.

Then I got injured in my sport. On top of that I had no money, so as soon as I could, I took warehouse work — any shift that was open.

I knew that wasn't where I was supposed to be. Late at night after shifts, I researched real estate strategies until I found wholesaling. I didn't have a plan B. I just refused to settle.

04 — The first close

February 4th, 2025. $61,000.

On February 4th, 2025, I closed my first wholesale deal — $61,000 in commissions, escrow closed on the 28th. That number changed what I believed was possible.

I poured it right back into marketing, ads, and my schooling, and went all in. By the end of the year I'd made over $160,000 wholesaling. I'd figured out how to generate deals — which is really just marketing under a different name.

The reward is nice. The reason I keep going is the work.

05 — The pivot

Deals are inconsistent. Skills aren't.

Wholesaling paid, but the deals were inconsistent and the industry had too much drama. I knew it wasn't what I'd do forever. What I really had was a skill: I could get anyone customers.

So I went back to my first mentor and offered to market his private security company — the one that started as word-of-mouth. We turned it into a brand that lands high-profile clients, from recording artists to national social creators. That's when it clicked: fixing growth for service businesses is the thing I'm meant to do.

Now
